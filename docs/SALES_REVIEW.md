## Pattern Analysis: What the Data Shows

### Your Strengths (keep doing these)

1. **You're a great listener.** Prospects talk 60-70% of the time on your calls. They share real pain points, competitive intel, and industry context. This is rare and valuable.
2. **The JK Equities story sells.** Every time you tell the story — "his inbox was getting blasted, we built an AI analyst, it broke for 3 hours and he called us freaking out" — prospects lean in. It's concrete, relatable, and proves value.
3. **You build rapport fast.** People genuinely like talking to you. Multiple prospects offered intros, follow-ups, and future collaboration.
4. **You're learning the industry in real-time.** After 10 calls, you understand deal flow, buy boxes, broker dynamics, Argus/Yardi lock-in, and AMI thresholds. This knowledge compounds.

### Your Weaknesses (fix these immediately)

1. **No positioning statement.** You introduce yourself as "figuring things out" and "we don't know real estate." This destroys credibility. Fix your intro (see Playbook below).
2. **No pricing, ever.** Across 10 calls and 40+ calls this month, you haven't named a price once. Prospects can't buy what doesn't have a price.
3. **No qualification.** You don't ask about budget, decision-making authority, or timeline. Multiple calls were with people who explicitly told you they can't approve spending (RJ at Caspi, RJ deferring to Josh; Matthew deferring to Necroman).
4. **Too many directions per call.** You pitch deal screening, underwriting, tax liens, zoning, probate, absentee owners, tenant communication, project management, and income verification — sometimes in the same call. The prospect doesn't know what you're selling.
5. **Weak closes.** "I'll send a follow-up email" is your default. It's passive, it puts the ball in your court to chase, and it gives the prospect no commitment to make.
6. **Underselling / self-deprecation.** "We're not making money" / "We're pretty vague" / "We don't know real estate" / "The goal isn't to go buy ribeyes." Stop telling people not to buy from you.

## The Playbook: 7 Changes to Make This Week

### 1. Fix your intro (60 seconds, memorize it)

**Stop saying:**
> "We're trying to figure out what to do next. We don't know real estate, we know software. We're looking for problems."

**Start saying:**
> "We're Frontstep. We build AI automation for commercial real estate firms. Our core product is a deal screening assistant — it monitors your acquisitions inbox, screens every broker blast against your buy box, extracts deal data, and delivers a daily digest. We're working with a client doing 100+ broker emails a day and it's saving them 15+ hours a week. We also build custom data intelligence tools — public records analysis, deal sourcing, underwriting automation. I'd love to hear about your process and see if there's a fit."

That's it. Confident, specific, value-oriented.

### 2. Create a one-page pricing sheet

Two offerings:

**Deal Screener — $X,000/month**
- Inbox monitoring via Microsoft Graph
- Custom buy box configuration
- Daily deal digest at your preferred time
- Broker quality tracking
- Setup in 2 weeks

**Custom Intelligence Project — $Y,000/week (4-8 week engagement)**
- Weekly check-ins
- Scoped deliverable (data platform, sourcing tool, underwriting automation)
- Dedicated engineering team

Pick prices that feel uncomfortable — you're replacing $150-250K/yr analysts. Anchor accordingly. You can always negotiate down; you can never negotiate up from "we're flexible."

### 3. Qualify in the first 5 minutes

Before you demo, before you pitch, ask:

- **"Who on your team would be involved in a decision to adopt something like this?"** (Authority)
- **"What are you spending on this today — whether it's analyst time, software, or manual hours?"** (Budget/Value)
- **"What's your timeline — is this something you'd want to move on this quarter?"** (Timeline)
- **"What happens if you don't solve this?"** (Consequence/Urgency)

If the answers are "I don't make that decision," "we don't have budget," and "no rush" — you're talking to the wrong person. Politely redirect to the decision maker or move on.

### 4. Always demo live

If the prospect can't see your screen, reschedule. Period. "This really needs a screen share to make sense — can we find 20 minutes when you're at your desk?" The BRIDGE call was a missed opportunity because Sean was on his phone. Your product is visual and impressive — don't describe it, show it.

### 5. Close with a specific next step — BEFORE hanging up

**Stop saying:** "I'll send a follow-up email"

**Start saying:** "Based on what you've told me, I think [specific product] would save you [specific value]. I'll send a one-page proposal with pricing by [day]. Can we schedule 20 minutes on [day] to review it with [decision maker name]?"

Book the next meeting before you hang up. If they won't commit to a next meeting, that tells you something about their level of interest — and that's useful information too.

### 6. Lead with the deal screener on every call

Even if the prospect has a different primary pain point, show the deal screener in the first 10 minutes. Why:
- It's built, it works, someone's paying for it
- It demonstrates competence and credibility for everything else you might propose
- Seeing a working product changes the conversation from "tell me about your problems" to "here's what we can do for you"
- It anchors the relationship around a real product, not vague consulting

After the demo, THEN explore whether their specific pain is the screener, a custom project, or something else.

### 7. Get Jordan Karlik to sell for you

Jared from Aurora said it plainly: "Demo should feature a real estate professional, not a technical co-founder." Jordan is your power user. He's the one who called you when the tool broke for 3 hours. Ask him to:
- Record a 2-minute video testimonial
- Join 1-2 prospect calls per month to describe his experience
- Give a quote you can use in proposals: "Before Frontstep, I was drowning in 100+ broker emails a day. Now I wake up to a sorted inbox and a deal summary at 6am."

One credible user testimonial is worth more than 10 of your demo calls.
